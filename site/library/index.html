1Purpose

Is this worth building, and what would prove it?

Move on when there is a named owner, a decision this will change, and a measure of success agreed before building.

  1. Problem-Based Software Requirements Specification

    Rafael Gorski M. Souza and Paulo Cézar Stadzisz, Revista Eletrônica de Sistemas de Informação · 2016

    Framework · Stable · also Design

    On the radar: Problem-based requirements (Trial)

    Why it matters
    Starts from named customer problems, not requested features, and derives customer needs and requirements from them. In an AI programme, every capability, including each agent, should then trace to a problem it solves.
    What to check
    Confirm every requirement derives from a customer need, and each need from a named customer problem; cut the rest. MIT-licensed agent skill: github.com/RafaelGorski/Problem-Based-SRS.

  2. Rules of Machine Learning: Best Practices for ML Engineering

    Martin Zinkevich, Google · living document

    Guide · Stable · also Design

    On the radar: Baseline before model (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Its first rule is not to be afraid to launch without machine learning. That sets the right opening question for any sponsor: what simple baseline must the AI beat to justify its cost?
    What to check
    Ship a heuristic or non-ML baseline first, instrument it, and require every model to beat it on the same metric.

  3. Hidden Technical Debt in Machine Learning Systems

    D. Sculley et al., NeurIPS · 2015

    Paper · Stable · also Operate

    On the radar: Feedback-loop mapping (Trial)

    Why it matters
    The ML code is a small fraction of a production ML system; the surrounding data, configuration and serving code carry most of the long-term cost. Budget for that, not for the demo.
    What to check
    Inventory undeclared consumers, data dependencies and feedback loops around each model before committing to scale it.

  4. Building effective agents

    Anthropic · 2024

    Guide · Stable · also Design

    On the radar: Workflows before autonomous agents (Adopt), Multi-agent decomposition (Assess)

    Why it matters
    Separates predefined workflows from autonomous agents and argues for the simplest design that works. The publisher flags that the tooling described has since changed; read it for the design reasoning.
    What to check
    For each proposed agent, document why a fixed workflow would not meet the requirement before granting it open-ended control.

  5. Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks

    Thomas Kwa et al., METR · 2025

    Paper · Evolving · also Design

    On the radar: Task time horizons (Assess)

    Why it matters
    Measures capability as the length of task, in skilled human time, that a model completes half the time. A sober way to set scope: delegate work inside that horizon, not beyond it.
    What to check
    Estimate how long your target tasks take a skilled person and compare that with the published time horizon of the model you plan to use.

  6. "Everyone wants to do the model work, not the data work": Data Cascades in High-Stakes AI

    Nithya Sambasivan et al., ACM CHI · 2021

    Paper · Stable · also Design

    Why it matters
    In interviews with 53 practitioners, 92% had met compounding data failures that surfaced late. The study ties them to practices that undervalue data work, which leaders can change.
    What to check
    Log when each data issue was introduced and when it was detected; the gap is your cascade exposure.

2Design

Does it work on real data, measured honestly?

Move on when results on real data beat a simple baseline, and the failures are written down, not only the average.

  1. τ-bench: A Benchmark for Tool-Agent-User Interaction in Real-World Domains

    Shunyu Yao et al. · 2024

    Paper · Stable

    On the radar: Repeated-trial reliability (pass^k) (Trial), Benchmark scores as launch evidence (Hold)

    Why it matters
    Introduces pass^k: the chance an agent succeeds on every one of k repeated attempts at the same task. A pilot that passes once is a demo; consistency is what customers experience.
    What to check
    Run each evaluation task several times and report pass^k alongside the single-run success rate.

  2. AI Agents That Matter

    Sayash Kapoor et al. · 2024

    Paper · Stable · also Purpose

    On the radar: Baseline before model (Adopt), Model cascades and routing (Trial), Cost reported with accuracy (Adopt), Benchmark scores as launch evidence (Hold)

    Why it matters
    Shows that agent evaluations often ignore cost and lack proper holdout sets, so headline accuracy can mislead. Ask for accuracy and cost together, measured on tasks the agent has not seen.
    What to check
    Plot every candidate agent on cost versus accuracy, including simple baselines, and require results on a held-out task set.

  3. Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena

    Lianmin Zheng et al., NeurIPS · 2023

    Paper · Stable

    On the radar: LLM judges calibrated to human grades (Trial), LLM judges without human calibration (Hold)

    Why it matters
    Strong models can approximate human preference ratings, which makes evaluation at scale affordable. The paper also documents position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases that must be controlled before trusting the scores.
    What to check
    Swap answer order, vary length and use a different model family as judge; report agreement with a human-labelled sample.

  4. Who Validates the Validators? Aligning LLM-Assisted Evaluation of LLM Outputs with Human Preferences

    Shreya Shankar et al. · 2024

    Paper · Stable · also Operate

    On the radar: LLM judges calibrated to human grades (Trial), LLM judges without human calibration (Hold)

    Why it matters
    Finds that people refine their evaluation criteria while grading outputs, so automated graders must be revisited as understanding grows. Evaluation is a managed asset, not a one-time setup.
    What to check
    Version your grading criteria and LLM-judge prompts, and re-measure their agreement with human grades whenever criteria change.

  5. Underspecification Presents Challenges for Credibility in Modern Machine Learning

    Alexander D'Amour et al., JMLR · 2022

    Paper · Stable · also Launch

    On the radar: Benchmark scores as launch evidence (Hold)

    Why it matters
    Models with equally strong test scores can behave very differently in deployment. A strong pilot result does not, on its own, predict production behaviour.
    What to check
    Evaluate candidate models on shifted and subgroup data that the pilot test set does not represent.

  6. Inspect

    UK AI Security Institute and Meridian Labs · living document

    Framework · Evolving · also Launch

    On the radar: Versioned open evaluation harness (Trial)

    Why it matters
    An open-source evaluation framework co-developed by the UK AI Security Institute, covering coding, agentic and reasoning tasks. A shared, inspectable harness makes results reproducible and comparable across model vendors.
    What to check
    Express pilot evaluations as versioned Inspect tasks so the same suite reruns unchanged on every model or prompt change.

  7. Datasheets for Datasets

    Timnit Gebru et al., Communications of the ACM · 2021

    Paper · Stable · also Govern

    On the radar: Model cards and datasheets (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    A standard set of questions about how a dataset was collected, labelled and intended to be used. Without those answers, no one can say whether pilot data represents production.
    What to check
    Require a datasheet for every evaluation and fine-tuning dataset, including its collection process, known gaps and permitted uses.

  8. Thinking in Systems: A Primer

    Donella H. Meadows, edited by Diana Wright, Chelsea Green Publishing · 2008

    Book · Stable · also Scale

    On the radar: Feedback-loop mapping (Trial)

    Why it matters
    Shows how a system's structure of stocks, flows, feedback loops and delays drives its behaviour. AI systems that learn from their own outputs, or agents that call other agents, create exactly such loops.
    What to check
    Draw the feedback loops the system creates, including outputs that return as inputs, and name the delay and the limit on each.

3Launch

Can we ship it safely to real users?

Move on when a staged rollout, a tested rollback and a human escalation path are live before the first user.

  1. Reliable Product Launches at Scale

    Google, Site Reliability Engineering (O'Reilly) · 2016

    Book · Stable

    On the radar: Canary releases for model and prompt changes (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Describes how Google turned launches into a repeatable discipline with a dedicated coordination role and a checklist. AI launches need the same rigour, plus questions specific to model behaviour.
    What to check
    Extend the launch checklist with rows for evaluation results, abuse testing, rollback of model and prompt versions, and cost limits.

  2. Canarying Releases

    Google, The Site Reliability Workbook (O'Reilly) · 2018

    Book · Stable · also Operate

    On the radar: Canary releases for model and prompt changes (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Expose a change to a small share of traffic, compare it against a control, then widen. For models and prompts, whose failures are statistical rather than crashes, this comparison is an essential safety net.
    What to check
    Route model and prompt changes through a canary with a control group and predefined quality, latency and cost thresholds.

  3. The ML Test Score: A Rubric for ML Production Readiness and Technical Debt Reduction

    Eric Breck et al., IEEE Big Data · 2017

    Paper · Stable · also Design

    Why it matters
    Twenty-eight specific tests and monitoring needs drawn from a wide range of production ML systems. It turns 'is this ready for production?' into a score a review board can track.
    What to check
    Score each system against the rubric at launch review and treat untested sections as open risks with named owners.

  4. OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications 2025

    OWASP Gen AI Security Project · 2024

    Guide · Stable

    On the radar: OWASP Top 10 launch review (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    The shared vocabulary security teams use for LLM application risk, from prompt injection to unbounded consumption. Asking which items a launch has mitigated is a fast, credible review question.
    What to check
    Map each of the ten risks to a named control or an accepted-risk decision in the launch review record.

  5. OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications for 2026

    OWASP Gen AI Security Project · 2025

    Guide · Evolving · also Scale

    On the radar: OWASP Top 10 launch review (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    A peer-reviewed list of the most critical security risks for autonomous and agentic AI systems. Use it alongside the LLM Top 10 once a system can act, not just answer.
    What to check
    Verify each agent runs with its own scoped identity and least-privilege tool permissions, never a shared human or service account.

  6. Guidelines for secure AI system development

    UK NCSC, US CISA and international partners · 2023

    Guide · Stable · also Govern

    Why it matters
    Joint guidance from national cyber security agencies, organised by lifecycle: secure design, development, deployment, and operation and maintenance. A security baseline a board can point to.
    What to check
    Confirm model weights, prompts and tool credentials are covered by the same asset inventory, access control and incident process as code.

  7. Expanding on what we missed with sycophancy

    OpenAI · 2025

    Postmortem · Stable · also Design

    On the radar: Blameless postmortems for AI incidents (Adopt), Continuous quality evaluation in production (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    A model update shipped on positive evaluations and A/B results despite expert testers saying it felt off, then was rolled back. OpenAI committed to treating behaviour issues as launch-blocking.
    What to check
    Give named expert reviewers authority to block a model release on qualitative findings, even when quantitative evaluations pass.

  8. Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction

    Saleema Amershi et al., Microsoft Research (ACM CHI) · 2019

    Paper · Stable

    Why it matters
    Eighteen guidelines, validated with 49 design practitioners, for how AI features set expectations, handle mistakes and support correction. They shape how users experience the system on the days it is wrong.
    What to check
    Review the launch UI against the guidelines on efficient correction, efficient dismissal and explaining why the system did what it did.

  9. Adversarial Machine Learning: A Taxonomy and Terminology of Attacks and Mitigations

    NIST (NIST AI 100-2 E2025) · 2025

    Guide · Stable · also Scale

    On the radar: Injection-resistant agent design patterns (Trial)

    Why it matters
    NIST's reference taxonomy of attacks on predictive and generative AI, including direct and indirect prompt injection and the security of agents. One vocabulary for security, engineering and risk teams.
    What to check
    Use its attack categories as the headings of your AI threat model so findings map to a public reference.

4Scale

Can it grow in users, agents and cost without losing control?

Move on when the cost of a completed task is known, and the platform enforces limits on spend and agent loops.

  1. Model Context Protocol Specification (2026-07-28)

    Model Context Protocol project (LF Projects) · 2026

    Specification · Evolving · also Launch

    On the radar: OAuth to the current security BCP (Adopt), Model Context Protocol (Trial)

    Why it matters
    An open protocol for connecting AI applications to tools and data. Standardising on one protocol reduces bespoke integration work and gives security teams a single interface to review.
    What to check
    Pin the protocol revision your servers support and review its authorization section before exposing any MCP server beyond localhost.

  2. Agent2Agent (A2A) Protocol Specification

    A2A Project, Linux Foundation · 2026

    Specification · Evolving

    On the radar: Agent2Agent protocol (Assess)

    Why it matters
    An open protocol for agents built by different teams or vendors to discover each other and delegate tasks. It matters once agents multiply and must cooperate across organisational boundaries.
    What to check
    Treat each Agent Card as an interface contract: version it, sign it, and review its declared skills and security schemes.

  3. The Tail at Scale

    Jeffrey Dean and Luiz André Barroso, Communications of the ACM · 2013

    Paper · Stable

    On the radar: Tail latency per agent step (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Brief latency spikes that are harmless in small systems come to dominate performance at scale. Agent workflows that chain many model and tool calls inherit this problem directly.
    What to check
    Measure p99 latency per step and end to end for agent runs, and set per-call timeouts and hedging where steps are idempotent.

  4. Addressing Cascading Failures

    Google, Site Reliability Engineering (O'Reilly) · 2016

    Book · Stable · also Operate

    On the radar: Unbudgeted automatic retries (Hold)

    Why it matters
    Explains how overload in one component spreads through retries and dependencies until a whole service fails. Agents that retry automatically can turn a model-provider slowdown into exactly this.
    What to check
    Cap retries with budgets and jittered backoff, propagate deadlines through agent steps, and load-test the provider-throttled case.

  5. FrugalGPT: How to Use Large Language Models While Reducing Cost and Improving Performance

    Lingjiao Chen, Matei Zaharia and James Zou · 2023

    Paper · Stable

    On the radar: Model cascades and routing (Trial), Cost reported with accuracy (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Routing each query through a cascade, cheaper models first and stronger ones only when needed, matched the best single model at up to 98% lower cost. Model choice is a per-request decision.
    What to check
    Log which model served each request and at what cost, then test whether a cheaper first-pass model meets the quality bar.

  6. FinOps for AI Overview

    FinOps Foundation · living document

    Guide · Evolving · also Govern

    On the radar: Cost and calls per completed task (Trial)

    Why it matters
    Vendor-neutral practice for allocating and controlling AI spend, including token-based pricing and scarce GPU capacity. AI costs move faster and reach more teams than typical cloud spend.
    What to check
    Tag every model call with team, product and use case so cost per outcome can be reported, not just total spend.

  7. Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?

    Mert Cemri et al. · 2025

    Paper · Stable · also Design

    On the radar: Workflows before autonomous agents (Adopt), Multi-agent decomposition (Assess)

    Why it matters
    A taxonomy of fourteen failure modes from expert-annotated traces, grouped into system design, misalignment between agents, and task verification. Adding agents adds coordination risk, not only capability.
    What to check
    Classify failed multi-agent runs with the paper's taxonomy, and require an explicit verification step before any agent marks a task complete.

  8. Design Patterns for Securing LLM Agents against Prompt Injections

    Luca Beurer-Kellner et al. · 2025

    Paper · Stable · also Launch

    On the radar: Injection-resistant agent design patterns (Trial), Controls enforced outside the model (Trial)

    Why it matters
    Proposes agent designs with provable resistance to prompt injection by limiting what an agent can do after reading untrusted input. Each trades some utility for security, a choice the business should own.
    What to check
    For each agent, identify where untrusted content enters and which pattern, such as plan-then-execute, constrains actions after that point.

  9. Best Current Practice for OAuth 2.0 Security

    IETF (RFC 9700) · 2025

    Standard · Stable · also Launch

    On the radar: OAuth to the current security BCP (Adopt), Model Context Protocol (Trial), Per-agent identity and least privilege (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Agents increasingly act on delegated credentials, and agent protocols such as MCP authorise through OAuth. This is the IETF's current security baseline for OAuth deployments.
    What to check
    Confirm agent access tokens are audience-restricted and sender-constrained, and that public-client refresh tokens are rotated or sender-constrained.

5Operate

Can we see, fix and improve it in production?

In control when every request leaves one connected trace, incidents are reviewed, and each lesson becomes an evaluation.

GN-0001 · Draft Agentic observability , original note

  1. Semantic conventions for generative AI systems

    OpenTelemetry · living document

    Specification · Evolving · also Scale

    On the radar: OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions (Trial), Telemetry only in a vendor's console (Hold), Cost and calls per completed task (Trial), Capturing prompts and outputs by default (Hold)

    Why it matters
    The open standard for recording model calls, agent steps, tool use and token usage as telemetry. It keeps observability portable across vendors. Still marked Development, so expect changes.
    What to check
    Emit gen_ai spans and token-usage metrics for every model and tool call, and record which convention version each service emits.

  2. Trace Context

    W3C · 2021

    Standard · Stable · also Scale

    On the radar: W3C Trace Context across agent hops (Adopt), OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions (Trial), Telemetry only in a vendor's console (Hold)

    Why it matters
    The web standard for carrying a trace identifier across services. When one user request passes through several agents, tools and vendors, it is what lets you reconstruct the whole path.
    What to check
    Confirm traceparent headers propagate through every agent hop, tool call and third-party API, including asynchronous queues.

  3. Dapper, a Large-Scale Distributed Systems Tracing Infrastructure

    Benjamin H. Sigelman et al., Google · 2010

    Paper · Stable

    On the radar: W3C Trace Context across agent hops (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Google's account of ubiquitous, low-overhead tracing across production services. Its lesson holds for agents: tracing pays off only when it is everywhere and cheap enough to leave on.
    What to check
    Decide your trace sampling policy explicitly; keep full traces for errors, high-cost agent runs and sampled quality reviews.

  4. Service Level Objectives

    Google, Site Reliability Engineering (O'Reilly) · 2016

    Book · Stable · also Launch

    On the radar: Tail latency per agent step (Adopt), Answer-quality SLOs (Trial)

    Why it matters
    Defines reliability through indicators and objectives that reflect what users notice, and makes the target explicit. For AI systems, answer quality belongs beside latency and availability.
    What to check
    Define at least one quality SLI per AI feature, such as graded answer acceptability on sampled traffic, with an explicit SLO target.

  5. Postmortem Culture: Learning from Failure

    Google, Site Reliability Engineering (O'Reilly) · 2016

    Book · Stable · also Govern

    On the radar: Blameless postmortems for AI incidents (Adopt), Shared AI incident disclosure (Assess)

    Why it matters
    Blameless postmortems turn incidents into durable fixes and shared knowledge. AI incidents, such as harmful outputs or quality regressions, deserve the same written review as outages.
    What to check
    Add AI-specific triggers to the postmortem policy: a quality regression, a harmful output reaching users, or an agent acting outside its scope.

  6. A postmortem of three recent issues

    Anthropic · 2025

    Postmortem · Stable

    On the radar: Blameless postmortems for AI incidents (Adopt), Continuous quality evaluation in production (Adopt), Answer-quality SLOs (Trial)

    Why it matters
    Three infrastructure bugs intermittently degraded responses, and the evaluations in place did not capture what users were reporting. Quality can regress in production through infrastructure alone.
    What to check
    Run quality evaluations continuously against true production endpoints, and keep a privacy-safe path to investigate user-reported bad outputs.

  7. How to Measure Anything: Finding the Value of Intangibles in Business, 3rd Edition

    Douglas W. Hubbard, Wiley · 2014

    Book · Stable · also Purpose

    On the radar: Value-of-information measurement (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Treats measurement as reducing uncertainty for a decision, not reaching certainty, and uses the value of information to choose what to measure. It answers the claim that an AI system's benefits are intangible.
    What to check
    For each production metric, name the decision it informs and estimate what better information is worth; retire metrics that inform no decision.

6Govern

Who is accountable, and can we prove it?

In control when every system has an accountable owner and a record of what it did, on whose authority and why.

  1. Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence (Artificial Intelligence Act)

    European Parliament and Council of the European Union · 2024

    Regulation · Evolving · also Launch

    On the radar: EU AI Act risk classification (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    EU law that tiers obligations by risk and places duties on deployers as well as providers. Obligations phase in over several years; establish which apply to your systems, and from when.
    What to check
    Classify each AI system against Article 6 and Annex III, and confirm logging and human-oversight duties for any high-risk use.

  2. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 Information technology — Artificial intelligence — Management system

    ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 · 2023

    Standard · Stable

    On the radar: ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    The certifiable management-system standard for AI, built on the same structure as ISO/IEC 27001. It gives boards an auditable answer to who owns AI risk, how it is assessed, and how it improves.
    What to check
    Map existing AI controls to the standard's Annex A and record gaps before deciding whether to pursue certification.

  3. ISO/IEC 42005:2025 Information technology — Artificial intelligence (AI) — AI system impact assessment

    ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 42 · 2025

    Standard · Stable · also Purpose

    On the radar: AI impact assessment before build (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Guidance on assessing how an AI system may affect individuals and society, and when in the lifecycle to do it. Done early, it shapes scope; done late, it becomes paperwork.
    What to check
    Run the impact assessment before build and again at each material change, and link results to the risk register.

  4. Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)

    NIST (NIST AI 100-1) · 2023

    Framework · Stable · also Purpose

    On the radar: NIST AI RMF and Generative AI Profile (Adopt), ISO/IEC 42001 AI management system (Adopt), AI impact assessment before build (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    A voluntary framework organised around four functions: govern, map, measure and manage. Useful as common language between risk, legal and engineering. NIST has announced it is being revised.
    What to check
    Map each AI system's controls to the four functions and name an accountable owner for every gap.

  5. Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework: Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile

    NIST (NIST AI 600-1) · 2024

    Framework · Stable · also Launch

    On the radar: NIST AI RMF and Generative AI Profile (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    Applies the AI RMF to generative AI, defining risks novel to or worsened by it, such as confabulation and value-chain integration, with suggested actions. A practical basis for governance reviews.
    What to check
    Check the launch risk register covers each of the profile's risk categories, with an owner and a chosen action or explicit acceptance.

  6. Practices for Governing Agentic AI Systems

    Yonadav Shavit et al., OpenAI · 2023

    Paper · Evolving · also Scale

    On the radar: Per-agent identity and least privilege (Adopt), Controls enforced outside the model (Trial)

    Why it matters
    Defines the parties in an agent's lifecycle and proposes initial practices such as constraining actions, requiring approval, attributability and interruptibility. A starting map of who is accountable when an agent acts.
    What to check
    For each production agent, confirm actions are attributable to a principal and that a tested shutdown path exists.

  7. Models as Insider Risks in the Super Intelligence Era

    Satya Nadella, sn scratchpad · 2026

    Guide · Evolving · also Operate

    On the radar: Per-agent identity and least privilege (Adopt), Controls enforced outside the model (Trial), Shared AI incident disclosure (Assess), Human approval as the only safeguard (Hold), Shifting accountability to the model or provider (Hold)

    Why it matters
    Treats frontier models as insider risks: identity, limited privileges, logged activity and containment, enforced outside the model. A provider's assurances do not transfer responsibility; authority over intelligence stays with the deployer.
    What to check
    Confirm access and action controls sit outside the model, every model action leaves tamper-proof evidence, and an authorised person can pause it mid-task.

  8. Model Cards for Model Reporting

    Margaret Mitchell et al., FAT* 2019 · 2019

    Paper · Stable · also Launch

    On the radar: Model cards and datasheets (Adopt)

    Why it matters
    A short, standard record of what a model is for, how it was evaluated, and where it should not be used. Write one before launch, not after an incident.
    What to check
    Maintain a card for each deployed model or agent, including disaggregated evaluation results and explicitly out-of-scope uses.

  9. Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149

    Civil Resolution Tribunal of British Columbia · 2024

    Postmortem · Stable · also Launch

    On the radar: Shifting accountability to the model or provider (Hold)

    Why it matters
    A tribunal held an airline liable for its website chatbot's incorrect advice, rejecting the idea that the chatbot was responsible for its own actions. A small claim, but a clear signal.
    What to check
    Ensure customer-facing AI answers on policy, price or entitlement are grounded in the authoritative source and logged for later review.

  10. Ironies of automation

    Lisanne Bainbridge, Automatica · 1983

    Paper · Stable · also Operate

    On the radar: Human approval as the only safeguard (Hold)

    Why it matters
    Automation erodes the skills and attention people need to supervise it and take over when it fails. Every 'human in the loop' promise for agents has to be designed against this.
    What to check
    For each human approval step, measure approval rates and review time; near-total approval with seconds of review signals rubber-stamping.
