About this site

Large AI programs fail less often on model quality than on the things around the model: how work is decomposed, how agents are observed, how cost and risk are governed, and how decisions made in a steering committee survive contact with production.

The map

The map divides the life of an AI system into six stages, from the first decision to accountable operation: Purpose, Design, Launch, Scale, Operate and Govern. Each stage is defined by the question it has to answer. Govern is drawn last, but it applies from the first decision.

Notes are written for the people who make those decisions. Each one is grounded in a public engineering standard or specification, explained without jargon, and closed with requirements that can be adopted as written. The aim is guidance an executive can act on and an engineer can check.

How sources are charted

Every source in the library is pinned to the one or two stages where it matters most, the first being where it is drawn on the map. Each carries two lines: why it matters, for the person who decides, and what to check, for the person who builds.

Shape
  • Rule standards, specifications and regulations
  • Practice frameworks, guides and books
  • Evidence papers and postmortems
Fill
  • Stable settled ground
  • Evolving published, still changing
  • Draft not yet settled

Being specific about maturity is part of the guidance. A program built on evolving ground should plan for change rather than discover it.

The radar

The radar is a second reading of the same ground. The map says when something matters in the life of an AI system; the radar says how far to rely on it. Each practice sits in one of four rings, from Adopt to Hold, and is backed by sources in the library. The placements are editorial judgement, and each edition marks what moved.

Two voices

The map, the radar and the library are written to be impersonal: they are a reference, and they should read the same whoever opens them. The home page is the opposite. It is my own take on building agents and AI systems, with dated lab notes on what I'm testing and what I'd watch out for. Across the site, vermilion marks my own writing and ultramarine marks the reference.

Colophon

Titles are lettered in Barlow Condensed Light, a slightly rounded condensed grotesk; plate lettering is set in Jost, a geometric typeface in the Futura line. Both are self-hosted under the SIL Open Font License. Reading text uses your system's sans serif. The pages are static HTML generated by a zero-dependency Node script from JSON files, and they read in full without JavaScript. There is no tracking and no request to any third party.

The mountains are generative. My surname, Górski, means "of the mountains" in Polish, so a small script draws each part of the site its own summit from a seed: contour lines seen from above behind the page titles and around the radar, and one ridge seen from the side that the map's six stages climb in order. They are drawn once, when the site is built, as plain images.

The site was built with coding agents: several sessions working in parallel on sources, design and the radar, each on its own branch, with a build and a link check deciding what gets published. The source is on GitHub.
