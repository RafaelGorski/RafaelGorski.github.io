About

Large AI programs fail less often on model quality than on the things around the model: how work is decomposed, how agents are observed, how cost and risk are governed, and how decisions made in a steering committee survive contact with production.

The map

The map divides the life of an AI system into six stages, from the first decision to accountable operation: Frame, Pilot, Launch, Scale, Operate and Govern. Each stage is defined by the question it has to answer. Govern is drawn last, but it applies from the first decision.

Notes are written for the people who make those decisions. Each one is grounded in a public engineering standard or specification, explained without jargon, and closed with requirements that can be adopted as written. The aim is guidance an executive can act on and an engineer can check.

How sources are charted

Every source in the library is pinned to the one or two stages where it matters most, the first being where it is drawn on the map. Each carries two lines: why it matters, for the person who decides, and what to check, for the person who builds.

Shape
  • Rule standards, specifications and regulations
  • Practice frameworks, guides and books
  • Evidence papers and postmortems
Fill
  • Stable settled ground
  • Evolving published, still changing
  • Draft not yet settled

Being specific about maturity is part of the guidance. A program built on evolving ground should plan for change rather than discover it.
