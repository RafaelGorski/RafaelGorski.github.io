Gear check for the climb

The route shows when something matters. The radar shows what to carry: Adopt is what to take on the climb, Trial what to test on the approach, Assess what to scout ahead, and Hold what to leave behind. Each of the 40 blips is backed by sources in the field guide.

Ring
  1. Adopt Take on the climb. Use by default, because the evidence is consistent and the practice holds up at production scale.
  2. Trial Test on the approach. Use on a real workload with an owner and an exit plan; sound, but not yet settled.
  3. Assess Scout ahead. Study it and test it in isolation; it may matter, but evidence does not yet justify production use.
  4. Hold Leave behind. Avoid, and plan to retire where present; the evidence shows it fails, misleads or hides risk.
Mark
  • No change
  • New
  • Moved in toward Adopt
  • Moved out toward Hold

  1. 1

    1. Baseline before model

    Adopt · Techniques · New

    Measure a simple non-ML or single-call baseline first, and require any model or agent to beat it on the same task, metric and cost.

    Evidence

    2. Workflows before autonomous agents

    Adopt · Techniques · New

    6. Feedback-loop mapping

  3. 7

    7. Model cascades and routing

    Trial · Techniques · New

    Send each request to the cheapest model that meets the quality bar and escalate only when needed; one study matched the best single model at up to 98% lower cost.

    Evidence

    8. Injection-resistant agent design patterns

  1. 10

    10. Unbudgeted automatic retries

    Hold · Techniques · New

    Agents and clients that retry without limits turn a slow dependency into overload. Cap retries with budgets and jittered backoff, and propagate deadlines through every step.

  1. 27

    27. LLM judges without human calibration

    Hold · Measures · New

    Grading criteria drift as people review outputs, and LLM judges carry documented biases, including self-enhancement. Without a human-graded check, scores cannot be trusted to track quality.

    Evidence
    • Who Validates the Validators?, Paper, stable

    28. Benchmark scores as launch evidence

    Hold · Measures · New

    38. Human approval as the only safeguard

    Hold · Governance · New

  2. 39

    39. Shifting accountability to the model or provider

    Hold · Governance · New

    A tribunal held an airline liable for its chatbot's advice, and provider assurances do not transfer responsibility. Plan as though every AI output is the organisation's own statement.

    Evidence
    • Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149, Postmortem, stable
